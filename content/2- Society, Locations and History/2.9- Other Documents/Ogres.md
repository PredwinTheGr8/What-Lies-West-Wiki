"Titans, the lot of them. Sea-sickened, pale skinned behemoths that would have won a war all on their own, but they weren't alone. And yet, despite their plate mail thick as a castle's wall, and dozens of Orcs running around their feet, they lost, the Ogres, the Orcs- the Catfolk. They all lost."

-Rakrak The Mouse, [[Scholars|scholar]]

The Ogres are a dying people, they once claimed [[Frigid South|The Frigid South]] as their home, and its mountains their kingdom. They stayed clear of [[Humans|Human]] kingdoms, of [[Dwarves|Dwarven]] holds and [[Elves|Elven]] citadels. Their cities were made from mammoth hides and bone scaffolding. Their weapons were spears and slings. Yet, despite their primitive ways the [[Goblins]] of [[Ramradga]] and the Human clergies of [[Church of Orpheus|The Church of Orpheus]] were never in any danger, never in any immediate threat from the Ogres.

The Ogres, and some of the southern Vikings lived in unity with one another. All until [[House Thaddeus]] nobles found out about the massive giants within the south, that is...

Greed, disgusting, hideous greed. Avarice, tempting and corrupting. Desire, horrible and evil. The nobles, with armies of soldiers at their back, began hunting Ogres for sport. Bars were filled with their tusks, libraries their severed heads, back alley apothecaries hung up their 'jewels'. At first, the Ogres fled further south, urging their Southling kin to flee with them. But the cold was too harsh, the winds to sharp. They could not follow.

The nobles, though, with money at their back, forged mighty rolling furnaces and dressed themselves in thick fur coats. The Ogres were pushed further and further south until they, too, could not bear the frost of the [[Polar Gates|polar gate]]. The Ice froze even metal into brittle glass, the flitting ice sprites sunk into bones and froze organs. Nothing, no matter how bulky, no matter how densely furred could survive here... but that meant that the humans could not follow, either. The Ogres found deep caves, kilns of insulation that could keep them heated in the arcane cold, and they disappeared.

Decades later, the [[Orc Revolution]] was near its start. The [[Orcs]], some of which bore banners for the hunting nobles as slaves, knew that the Ogres had fled south, and that for the Orcs to have any real chance of victory the Ogres would need to be recruited. A small group fled their encampment and found asylum in the lands of The Church. From there, they organized with odd jobs and gathering provisions until finally they were ready to endure  [[Frigid South|The Frigid South's]] bite. It was a long journey, many died- but the handful that pressed onwards through the flesh rending ice shards and soul freezing cold found what they were looking for- the tracks of an Ogre.

The revolutionaries pressed onward. One succumbed to frostbite, another starvation, a third to an icicle to the jugular. Finally, only two found it- the massive wooden wall blocking a gargantuan cave mouth hiding from the north-blowing winds. The two greenskins banged upon the wall for what seemed like hours, screaming to be let in. It felt like ages before a massive, pale hand reached through a crack and pulled the door open.

The Ogres listened to their plight- they stood 'round a massive bonfire, silently staring at the Orc duo as they spoke of the oncoming revolution- the mobilization of thousands of slaves desperate for vengeance and freedom. The Ogre leaders looked among each other for a moment, before nodding in near unison.

The Orcs, as the rulers of industry for the last several hundred years, were familiar with forging armor and weapons, and resizing them for larger or smaller soldiers. They had a plan- to make the Ogres immune to their one weakness- to make the Ogres invincible to everything that could ever be flung at them. 

Four. Four inches of solid iron, steel, bronze whatever could be found- but FOUR entire inches of metal armor nonetheless. Spears broke in half, claymores chipped into pieces, trebuchet volleys shattered on impact, the force of the blow absorbed by the dense blubbery fat of the  Ogre's stomachs. Sure, some weapons could still harm them- ballistae, heavier trebuchets and catapults, oil pits. But all these tactics rely on a stationary foe, or at least a sluggish one. Ogres, though, are anything but slow. Even in full plate each stride they took was ten of a mans, each running gate they made covered houses in distance with each quaking step.

Though not as big as the mighty [[Bath'kah]]]- fourteen to sixteen feet tall is still massive. The morale of humans and elves broke quickly as invincible titans charged their castles and ripped up their palisades by the roots. Spike traps were kicked into dust, Orcs in wicker cages were thrown over walls, [[Catfolk]] assassins lobbed by hulking muscles into the fourth story windows of nobles' houses. The Ogres were absolutely the reason for the Armageddon-ous march of the Orcs and their supporters.

So, then. How, despite all of this, did the Ogres, and by proxy the Orcs... loose? The [[Minotaurs]]. They came from their northern prison with promises of citizenship and coin- and wrought death unto the Green, Pale, and Fur-skinned masses of the Revolution. Even Human defectors and Dwarven kinsmen who supported the revolution lost their heads and families. The Minotaurs had brought flintlock weapons- mighty projectile firearms that pierced through the Ogre's armor as if made from soggy bread. 

The war was over in a month.

Though they earned their safety, and their right to live within the bounds of ex-[[Koross|Korosian]] territories, what was once a thousand Ogres is less than a hundred. Sad, weeping folk who have lost everything, and despite their best efforts their population only sinks. Less and less giants roam the world each year, and eventually the only ones left will be the ones to die alone- to die as an alien monster that children told stories of to scare each other. For you see, the Ogrekind lives forever, they do not die of old age- only illness or injury. They are very, very ill nowadays. Though they lived for a great deal in the Frigid South and grew acclimated to it, the pox of the Minotaurs was too strong for even their hardy biology. Their community was too closely knit, and after the war ended, all of them caught the cough.
