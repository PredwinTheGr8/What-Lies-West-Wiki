### [[Monster Callsigns|Callsigns]]-

- Detect good
- Chirps
- Rock Cairns

Goodfellows are small, beloved [[Monsters]] of [[The West]] that reside within both [[True Desert|The True Desert]] and [[Shattered Divide|The Shattered Divide]], though they remain incredibly rare in the former. Goodfellows, named by adventurer Markus Krauler for their cheering appearance, are small, two foot tall insects with hardened, buckler shaped shells covering them like a parasol. They possess blue-green shells that shine under the light of the Tyrannical Sun. Their little legs poke just shyly out from their shells, their eyes small beacons of white light that piece the fog of their common home.

Goodfellows are drawn to the heavy footfalls of well equipped adventurers. They run up to their feet with alarming, but whimsical speed before tilting their heads up to ensure the adventurer is, indeed, well armed. If a Goodfellow isn't happy with what they see, they will make several low clicks before running back off to their crevice, and the adventurer won't see any more Goodfellows until they take a shower, suggesting some kind of pheromone is at play.

But, if a Goodfellow likes what it sees- five or more adventurers with scars on their faces, blood covered weapons, and the smell of arcane power, they will begin to chirp rapidly, causing even more Goodfellows to emerge from their holes. The Goodfellows will then circle the party or lone adventurer for a short time as three of their own number run off to scout. Adventurers report that first experiences with a swarm of Goodfellows can be intimidating, but that eventually it becomes clear they mean absolutely no harm.

Eventually, the scouting Goodfellows will return with one of two sounds. One, a shrill, almost childlike scream, though resembling one of excitement rather than fear or pain. The other, the same low chirp as when they are disappointed with the kit of an adventurer. If the latter happens, many of the Goodfellows will lower their antennas in disappointment and retreat to their dens, though it is important to note in this instance that the group of Adventurers may still encounter further groups of Goodfellows. A small handful of Goodfellows may continue following the party for a short while- particularly if one among the adventurers number has pet them at all or given them treats.

The former noise, however, will cause the Goodfellows to begin running off in the direction the scouting bugs came from. They will frequently turn back to ensure that their party-of-choice is still following them as they guide them acrost the metal bridges and past the towering monoliths of graviturgy glyphs.

A wise party will follow a group of Goodfellows, as it means nothing short of fortune. The Goodfellows will guide the party to the den of a [[Wyrmlings|Wyrmling]]. The entrances are usually on the underside of the floating islands within [[Shattered Divide|The Shattered Divide]], making them impossible for the average party to find. But the Goodfellows are good for finding smaller caves that happen to lead straight to a den. Saving the specifics for their own archive, a Wyrmlings den is often full of ancient relics and gold. Needless to say the parts of a Wyrmling themself sell for a high price to alchemists.

Sometimes, its a false lead though, and a group of Goodfellows will lead to an empty, previously inhabited but already looted cave. Still, sometimes a relic or two has been left behind.

So then, what do the Goodfellows get out of this arrangement? Simple, the flesh of the Wyrmling ideally. The muscle and organs of a Wyrmling are charged with magic, which the Goodfellows actively seek to sustain themselves. And a Wyrmling just so happens to be big enough for a sizeable colony to feast to satiation.

Adventurers will often take an immediate rest after felling a Wyrmling, and as they sit the cheerful little bugs will round up and deliver individual scales and other inedible bits to their feeders. So then, the main question- who taught them to do this?

Well, that remains unknown. Since the first encounter with Goodfellows, they have always acted this way. Always guiding adventurers to treasure and the beasts guarding it. Some stories say that Goodfellows can be found anywhere, but confirmed reports from credible parties have only come from The Divide and The True Desert. 

Finally, the nature of their true biology. Goodfellows are certainly, without a doubt, fae. Their flesh is an inky black void, their eyes are shapes of white light that change with their emotions, bowing into upside down 'u's' when they're happy and separating into little dots when they're sad. They don't prompt reaction from spells that target only evil creatures- such as [[The Knights in Yellow]] or [[Death Knights]], and though they are certainly kind they are nor angels. And yet the spell detect good and evil detects them just fine.

Whatever the case, they remain cherished monsters of The West.

If, for some ungodly reason, you have chosen to attack a Goodfellow, they are weak creatures and die easily. But there are absolutely no reasons to attack them- as their shells are worthless and their flesh disintegrates upon death. The smell of a dead Goodfellow on a person's hands also activates the wrath of [[Chromatic Sentinels]], making it an even more foolish decision other than wasting time. Finally, it unleashes an arcane mark unto their killer, warning other Goodfellows to their nature forever, at least until a dispel curse spell is used.

Goodfellows are in the middle of the food chain within The Divide. They cannot be eaten but that doesn't stop some creatures from trying anyways such as [[Spectotums]]. Most small critters fall prey to Goodfellows, though, and of course they utilize adventurers to bring down larger monsters.
