### [[Monster Callsigns|Callsigns]]-

- Muffled laughter
- Infrequent Mind Whispers
- Sound of scarping metal
- Smell of Curses, Described As Vinegar
- Smell of Graviturgy, Described As Petrichor
- Smell of Archeoscript, Described As Caramel

Shamblers are [[Eldritch Energy|Eldritch Spawn]] that hail from [[The True Desert]] of [[The West]], with them being sighted frequently on the horizon bordering [[The City of Wyrms]] amassing in number. Shamblers often are compared to stretched out humans- with long, dangly limbs that barely keep the creatures upright. Their flesh is a sick, blood red hue and holds the consistency of clay or wax depending on age, with the substance hardening over time- eventually immobilizing particularly old specimens. Over this flesh, and artificial, dull white metal armor holds together the red flesh with numerous underlying arcane glyphs- namely two [[Archeoscript]] words; Callipse and Egon, and a [[Graviturgy]] word; Aaries. These magic runes are what even allows a Shambler to move, with the destruction of armor segments removing locomotion from their respective limb.

With a consistent height of nine feet between individual specimens, Shamblers tower over most [[adventurers]], staring down at them with numerous eyes covering their face. On their face, hidden under the armor, is a crude mouth incapable of articulated speech. The Shambler solely uses this mouth (and an unknown lung source) to laugh during slaughter. Whether this is voluntary or not, it serves to demoralize mortals as they slice into them with crude, rusty weapons- or at least it seems at the surface. The weapons are comprised of [[Eldritch Energy]]. Though heavily refined and safer than other sources, if a mortal were to wield one such weapon they would quickly lose their mind.

Shamblers are known most for their strange behavior regarding [[The City of Wyrms]]. When colonizers first started refitting the city before [[Adventurers|adventuring]] was authorized at full scale, several towns guard reported numerous times that at sunrise they could see numerous figures silhouetted against the dunes wielding weapons. Numerous times, the Guard captain- Elzroy Ishvey- came only to see nothing against the horizon. This went on until almost every guardsman reported seeing the figures- and though many thought it delirium it only took another week for them to be proven wrong.

The Shamblers attacked in the thousands. The under-prepared City of Wyrms couldn't hold back the Deeptide, and many lives were lost between the unarmed citizens, the towns guard and adventurers-to-be. While the City has recovered, and most Citizens nowadays likely do not even know of Shamblers, it requires regular exterminations every week to cull the number of Shamblers amassing outside the walls, lest they besiege again once they reach a critical mass.

So long as they are not accompanied by another, more intelligent Spawn such as perhaps a [[Monoliths|Monolith]]- a Shambler will remain in place until commanded otherwise or attacked by an outside force such as a mortal. Shamblers are incredibly dull unless controlled by another Spawn, with the most common "commissars" seen commanding them being [[The Things in The Dark, Null Figures|The Things in The Dark]] and [[Immolators]]. Each Spawn has their own methods and results when commanding Shamblers, with attacks led by Null Figures being far more cold and coordinated, and ones led by Immolators often being brash and more likely to fail. Immolators of course only started commanding Shamblers recently after their sudden and near-catastrophic introduction to [[The West]].

Without a way to track down their source, only speculation can be made as to the origins of The Shamblers. They have existed for as long as we have wondered what lies west, and if the legends of old are to be believed even once assailed ancient [[Koross]]- though in those stories they are never called 'Shamblers'.

In combat against a Shambler, avoiding over-headed attacks is key. The force with which they attack is difficult to block even through shields. Keeping light on your feet and striking the legs to bring the head into range of attack is key, as severing the head is the quickest way to demobilize a Shambler- as 'killing' a Shambler requires ritual, or complete incineration of its flesh.

As True Spawn, Shamblers do not need to eat, sleep or drink- and therefor do not play a part in the ecosystem. The Towns Guard humbly ask that adventurers 'show em what for' on encounter and help to mitigate their threat on the city.
