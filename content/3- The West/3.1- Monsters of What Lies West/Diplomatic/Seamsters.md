Seamsters are ruin-dwelling [[Legendary Monsters]] of [[The West]] that are a very rare find underneath the sands. Seamsters closely resemble eastern arachnids such as the Green-Gold Tarantula, with large-muscled legs and large hairs on their thorax. Seamsters can be found deep inside of any underground structure, though they prefer making their dens near sites of great historical significance. The den of a Seamster is full of webbing- the reason for their namesake- often spun into great tapestries and dyed with colors to tell stories, most often indecipherable to the adventurers that find them.

While no scholars have managed to make contact with a Seamster, adventurers desert wide have recounted thirty eight separate encounters over the forty or so years that expeditions have been authorized. Despite the repetitive encounters, little remains known of the motives behind Seamsters, though it is an absolute certainty to say they mean no harm, at least for now.

Seamsters speak in, according to the stories of adventurers, ancient Korosian language, likening to Skriek Whisper-Whisker's own speech. Still, the accent remains mostly impregnable for most adventurers. Seamsters are also known to speak in metaphors and double meanings. Below is a direct transcript written by adventurer Amelia Harrow. I will follow it with my own interpretation of the words.

"The tree withers and blooms as the wind whispers deeper thoughts into its yearning bark. The tree does not know it needs what it does not. It simply does. But the wind comes regardless, and its gusts flow over the bark and splinter it with churning gales. The tree regrows and reforms but is not the same, bark and trunk and leaf."

For a moment, I'd like you to imagine what it means. What does the seamster say? Please, the more thoughts the more alternatives may be found. Finished? Good then.

I believe this stands as a metaphor for mutation personally. The 'tree' is the body and the 'wind' is the essence of [[The Deep]]. The wind strips away the bark- the 'flesh' of the body- and when it regrows the whole tree is not the same, body, soul and mind- bark, trunk and leaves. I also have several other thoughts from my fellow Scholars below.

Largely, they share a sentiment that this is a metaphor for change- that the "wind" is other people or things that alter one's life. My personal favorite interpretation goes as follows.

"Life is unending and unceasing in its wearing of our fragile forms. Yet by some godly designs our form remains strong. Strain and contentment each is found in abundance and weathers away the more fragile body, that body of the internal designs. Those are such constructions as to be both profoundly fragile yet profoundly stable. Regrowth begets a new *internal* form every second of every minute of every hour of every day. The wind comes, the soul shatters and we begin again."

-Orn, [[Scholars|scholar]]

I must say that I prefer this interpretation over my own. Lets look at another then, shall we?

"The man in suit and tie offers an accord without using words. The deal is not wanted nor needed but cannot be denied. The deal makes others wary and fearful, the deal makes the world bleak. The kindly peasant offers to take the deal away. Others smile again, the world seems bright. The man in suit and tie is not pleased you have given away his gift."

This one eludes me for now. 'Suit and Tie' is rather strange though. Does it mean a suit of armor? And a tie? A knot?

Amelia Harrow, and by extension her party, have become very well acquainted with one particular Seamster that they have named Junibell. Thirteen of the above encounters with Seamsters are in fact all from Amelia Harrow's own repetitive talks with this Junibell. Seamsters seem to possess the power to attract adventurers by manipulating the same power that keeps them confused in [[The West]]. This blatant manipulation of The West's aura of utter confusion proposes that somehow Seamsters could be used to finally break whatever spell is causing this.

Back to Amelia. Her and her companions first made contact with Junibell either the day of or one close to the day [[The Annihilator]] sieged [[The City of Wyrms]]. Junibell spoke unto them:

"The wastes and waters of perpetuity crash upon hundreds. They scream as the abyss wakes and gnashes them unto paste. Here, you walk far from hale cries and dissonant whispers. Far from the Eye of Oblivion." 

It should be noted that Amelia had already gotten into the practice of quoting at this point. Regardless, this is obviously a reference to the active destruction occurring at the time within the city. This "eye of oblivion" is likely to be The Annihilator itself.

Amelia was putting her notepad back inside of her satchel when a bundle of [[Mire Weed]] she had harvested inside of [[The Blasted Basin|The Blasted Basin]] fell out and rolled to the feet of the Seamstress. Amelia went to retrieve it but the creature made a noise she had never heard, a sort of 'purr' she describes it. The creature picked up the Mire Weed and ground it into a rough paste, before putting the mixture onto a gash on its thorax. Followed with the below quote, partially obstructed because Amelia had to take her notebook back out again.

"-Thus the artist gleams with joy at her connoisseurs. She waves unto them and assures that her work will always be beautiful for them."

After this, Amelia Harrow and her fellows made a return to [[The City of Wyrms]], where of course they found out about the carnage that 'Junibell' had spoken of. After spending a month assisting with repairs the group finally made another trek out into the West, and immediately were manipulated back outside the ruins where they found Junibell. Entering, the ruins were noticeably far more tidy, much of the rubble on the floor had been glued back into the ceiling and walls with silken thread, and the floor itself had clearly been washed thoroughly. Upon entering the ruin's largest chamber- which previously held several [[The Knights in Yellow|Knights in Yellow]] that the party had dismantled- Junibell laid in wait for them.

Beneath her was a large charcuterie, filled with absolutely ancient aged cheeses and wines... Lucky. Junibell spoke again, and this time Amelia was more than ready to quote her.

"The Artist gleams again, to see her connoisseurs. She pleads them to take off their coats and partake in revelry of the King."

According the Harold Green, after this Junibell spoke much during their little 'tea party' but Amelia was too busy enjoying her food to record anything, and nobody else except Ezlan are illiterate except for writing their names. Orpheus damnit.

After the feast though, Junibell gifted Amelia what can only be described as the most luxurious, beautiful cloak ever made, spun by none other than the Seamstress herself. I keep seeing her walking around with it every day- pure white, golden embroidery, hefty weight. I think it keeps itself clean, and its definitely waterproof she always takes the hood up when it rains and she's dry afterwards. What I wouldn't give to just have a feel, it must be absolutely divine. Orpheus forgive my vanity.

Seamsters other than Junibell aren't so outwardly kind to adventurers, though that's not to say they're in any way rude or hostile, Junibell just seems to be an outlier in kindness.

Seamsters are self perpetual, meaning they are effectively immortal unless slain. How they sustain themselves has been asked, but as can be expected their answers are vague at best. Seamster's can be found wounded sometimes, suggesting that they have a foe of sorts, whether or not its a predator though is up for debate or whoever can decipher whatever it is they're trying to say.
