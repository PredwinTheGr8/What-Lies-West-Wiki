([[Build Togethers|549]] [[index|What Lies West?]])

"They scare me a bit if I'm to be honest. They're so tall, so spindly, so conscious. If any plant in [[The West]] was closest to enlightenment, it would be one of those grey-petalled freakish trees."

-Kask Way-Stalk, [[adventurers|adventurer]]

Voltaic Seekers, or as we will simply call them; seekers, are massive floral specimens found within [[The Voltaic Monolith]]'s domain. As manavores, Seekers earn a great deal of their energy by attracting [[Electromancy|electromancy]] via their [[Magic Patterns|Vermaus Pattern]]- a series of repeating, square shapes that endlessly loop over each other. Without sunlight or water, Voltaic Seekers are liable to be small to conserve energy- or at least one would suspect such a thing.

Seekers instead opt to dominate the skyline and grow as much surface area as possible, with massive "gills" like those of a fungi growing up the stalk of the plant's nearly thirty foot tall adult size. Whereas other manavores within the Monolith's range of power stay small to conserve their precious energy, the Seeker defies this standard to dominate the skies with massive canopies.

With the frequent and instantaneous movement of The Voltaic Monolith between lightning strikes, Seekers earn their namesake for turning to face The Monolith between massive bolts that conceal its movement. [[Electromancy|Electromancy's]] actions against physical space are little understood to this day, but seemingly the Seeker knows enough to predict the next location the Monolith will 'teleport' to.

The Vermaus pattern on the Seeker is incredibly refined as well, with the patterns smallest points being roughly 0.23 inches acrost instead of other plants one inch minimum gaps. The plant even fills up the spaces between the larger patterns with more, smaller patterns- an otherwise unseen utilization of space in other manavoring plants. The Seekers' size lends to their further development, as some ancient specimens reach titanic sizes that rival even the [[Weeping Titans]] of [[The Sorrowed Weald]]. With larger specimens, the 'plants' will even begin dragging metal scraps up their trunk and putting the pieces onto their tip, drawing in lightning for them to consume- though this process can take weeks as the only truly "mobile" part of a Seeker is it's 'face.'

The face of a Seeker is a large, flat, circular surface like that of a sunflowers- in fact I would say its possible that at one point the Seekers *were* sunflowers, or another similar plant at the very least. This 'face' holds some kind of crude vision system, allowing the Seeker to see and respond to the flashes coming from [[The Voltaic Monolith]], being the most likely tool from which the Seeker determines the next location the Monolith will appear. This face will also move to track the movements of close, [[Electromancy|electromancy]] specializing Mages. This has given the plants a reputation of stalking adventurers, though obviously it is merely the manavores inner mechanisms directing the movement and not any sort of consciousness.

Finally, the matter of territorial fights. As strange as that sounds, its true- Voltaic Seekers are territorial. With their roots, Seekers slowly move throughout the [[All Regions of The West|Region]] they call home. Scholars believe this behavior is to avoid distant warps from The Voltaic Monolith- predicting upwards of one thousand or so jumps and acting accordingly and avoiding being crushed when the Monolith suddenly occupies their space- a phenomenon called "Violent Repositioning Trauma", or simply V.R.T. During this time, if two or more Seekers cross paths they will engage in violent combat between one another- conducting massive amounts of energy to their face and firing it off in short beams of electric wrath. The Seeker with higher reserves usually overloads their competitor, resulting in a violent blue explosion as the natural [[Storm Batteries|Storm Battery]] combusts. This same mechanism is susceptible to mortal detonation as well- with at least two recorded 'tragedies' when adventurers tried to cut down a Seeker for profit, only for the internal energy to rapidly, and violently, release.

The leaves of a Seeker are edible, and make for good tea or other herbal cuisine when steeped. Ratfolk alone are capable of processing their densely starched tubers grown in their root structures.
