****"The Sorrowed Weald is a horrific kind of loneliness. I don't really know how to describe the feeling... but I'll try. It's like being home alone when your nan has gone out shopping or strolling. You feel free to do whatever you want in the empty, dark house. But you know, deep down that whatever you touch, she's gonna notice. That at any moment, she could come home and find you in the middle of stacking wine glasses on the table. That's what it's like in The Weald, constant worry that whatever you do something will find out, and that something will be angry."

-Harold Green ([[Adventurers|adventurer]]) during my first, and frankly terrible, expedition into [[The West]].

The Sorrowed Weald is a [[All Regions of The West|region]] of [[The West]] characterized by a blue mist and towering trees- known hauntingly as [[Weeping Titans|The Weeping Titans]]. These trees- second largest in the whole of [[Maernos]]- are the base layer of the entire ecosystem and therefor the prosperity of the region. Without the trees there would be no Weald, and without the Weald over ***one third*** of the species within The West would be missing a crucial part of their lifecycles or food sources.

With an incredibly diverse biosphere, it would take decades to categorize and catalogue every [[Monsters|monster]] within The Weald alone, from individual creatures to swarms of insects so large that you'd mistake them for a new type of soil instead of a mobile mass of life. Those who've tried to name every creature, like myself and Ashkram Who Speaks Strange, have had quite the task laid out Infront of them. There are, by my counts, at least one thousand individual species of beetle within The Weald- a number gathered by a cursory Chairolithi spell, a spell similar in function to something like Clairvoyance. This means that, unless a massive group of dedicated [[Scholars|scholars]] make their way to The Weald at once, we won't know every individual species by appearance for at least a thousand or so years.

For megafauna though, a much smaller number exists- a number we can discuss later. The reason is rather simple- The Weald demands specialization of its permanent residents. Water-resistance, food scrounging, cold resistance, exceptional olfactory systems, the ability to discern when a fight is necessary versus when it is not; the list goes on. Megafauna have a much harder time surviving into adulthood within The Weald than anywhere else, as its not only predators working to end their lives, but rivals, the weather, and even the plant life.

Starting with the cold.

Other than [[The Intra-Boreal Exclusion Zone|The Ibeks]], The Weald is the coldest region of [[The West]] during the night. With the constant moisture in the air, and the lack of it beyond the canopy, the water rapidly cools upon being misted outwards by a [[Weeping Titans|Weeping Titan]], and then sinks back down into the forest below. It only takes roughly an hour after dusk for the Weald to go from rather warm temperatures to dangerously cold ones. While the lower altitudes within The Weald remain above freezing- its not by much, and the further up you climb into the canopy the colder it gets, with The Weeping Titans freezing near-solid about halfway up their trunks, and giant shards of ice hanging precariously from their mighty boughs. Creatures that live in these canopies, or close to them, must either descend every night or adapt to survive the harsh temperatures.

The night is also the only time the "rainfall" of The Weald stops, or at least slows down. Some nocturnal creatures of The Weald are entirely reliant on this facet of the weather, and if it did keep raining during the night its likely that they would have a hard time hunting or scavenging due to their own lackluster skills in such things during rainfall. These nocturnal predators often have a much easier time finding prey than diurnal ones, though their prey usually knows that they are coming long before they arrive.

As day breaks, it takes a while before things in the trees start moving again. Roughly three hours or so after daybreak, the Weald will return to a semi-normal state; with water beginning to flow once more and the dormant life waking under the faint light piercing the leaves. Birds begin to sing, insects chirp, and larger monsters begin their mating calls or what have you as the torrent begins to pile up.

This brevity of quite as the water begins to melt is the only time many monsters within The Weald will make long-distance calls, like the [[Creeping Toads]] and [[Ouberinth]] who use this time to find a mate. Then, as the rain begins to drown out all other noise the creatures will go silent as the predators of day emerge to hunt.

Diurnal predators, and therefor their quarry, are incredibly resistant to the prevalent water of The Weald. While there are many amphibians to be seen there are still some cold blooded reptiles- wooly mammals, and stone-skinned curiosities to be found. Even some roaming creatures like [[Arcanosaurs]] that wish to enter the deepest recesses of The Weald cannot simply stroll in and merely hope to avoid disease from the waters that take up every step. 

A splendid example of over adaptation is the [[Walking Bastions]]. Walking Bastions posses dense plates of keratin and chitin that serve to completely insulate the lower extremities of the Bastion against the water. While yes, these plates do begin to rot, Walking Bastions have adapted to frequently shed the outer layers of these plates, losing roughly an inch per month according to my time spent in The Weald. I'll leave the specifics to their document I plan to make public in a fortnight.

Other creatures avoid the moisture by staying arboreal. [[Man-Fishers]], for as little as we know about them, always attack from above, or at least from a tall angle impossible from the ground. Man-Fishers likely rely on their arboreal nature, meaning that they live or at least primarily move through trees, to survive the dampness of The Weald. Man-Fishers have gone so far as to not touching the ground that they don't even need to manually retrieve their prey- with their harpoon-like protrusion reeling in anything caught in mere moments.

Next, the rain itself.

The Sorrowed Weald is constantly assailed by a torrent during the daytime hours of [[The West]]. This water cycles through the [[Weeping Titans]], where it is misted above the forest and then falls back down from the leaves to create dense pockets of drippage. Low altitude areas of The Weald are flooded every morning and drained every night as the highly effective roots of the Titans cycle the water over and over. These low-lying areas that clear up every night offer vital nutrients to scavengers as nuts and berries that are washed awry by the torrent during the day are gathered here and left for the taking by whatever creatures are brave enough to venture out at night.

During the Day the rain drowns out scents from creatures, like their dung or just natural odor- this means that diurnal predators that rely on smell must posses incredible olfactory systems to track their quarry through the downpour. An excellent example of this would be the ever-infamous [[Riphawgs]], which use massive and powerful nasal cavities to detect even the smallest fragment of a stench. During my expedition into The West with Harold Green's party it was the very presence of Riphawgs that stressed me out the most during my time spent inside of The Weald. These predators, in a group of roughly six individuals followed us for the entire two weeks we spent in the region and regularly probed our defenses at night. Girtz managed to kill one, but that only made the other five more irritable as they continued to track us the entire trip.

When they aren't hounding at [[adventurers]] Riphawgs will track down any number of prey items even through the densest rains around two thirds of the way through dawn, they are an unrelenting predator that cannot be evaded, but only driven off through intimidation or violence.

At night, when the rains stop predators and scavengers alike need dense coats or blubbery bodies to survive the near-freezing temperatures. Even if they do everything right a creature may simply be unlucky, and get skewered by a precarious icicle falling from the canopy above. I encountered one such sight as a colony of [[Vren]] feasted decadently upon the recent carcass of an [[Ouberinth]] with a large spire of ice rammed into its spinal cord.

[[Ouberinth]], meaning "Of Ivory" in elven, are premier scavengers of the night, with their blood red eyes glowing dimly, they tread with caution over every last shrub before sitting down and munching on a fruiting berry bush. Ouberinth, while relatively tame are still fearsome if provoked, and their predators, like [[Man-Fishers]] and [[Creeping Toads]] must move quietly and confidently to catch them by surprise, as during the night when they appear there is no rainfall to drown out the footsteps of the large predators that hunt them.

The point being with all these things is that generalists don't live long, or rather can't stay long if they're outsiders. It takes specialization and cunning to survive permanently within The Weald, a kind of specialization that would leave these creatures unable to survive anywhere else, save for the monolithic marsh of [[The Great Swamp|Moulugg]] if the arcane power was to be matched to [[The West|The West's]]. While, yes it is true that most [[All Regions of The West|regions]] of The West do in fact favor specialists in their zones, generalist fauna can still survive, or even in the humble [[Arcanosaurs|Arcanosaur's]] case even thrive.

Even the Flora must adapt to the extremes of The Weald. Take the least eye catching specimen of Flora within The Weald for instance, the humble [[Wander Moss]]. Wander Moss is a semi-mobile floral specimen that uses its roughly one inch per hour movement to crawl acrost the bark of the [[Weeping Titans]] during the night. The Wander Moss locates pockets of rotting wood and eats away the fouled bark during this time with mild acids. Notably this process allows the Weeping Titan to heal over the removed area, essentially disinfecting an infection over the trunk that is healed later. As day begins to roll around, the moss sends out smaller pockets to find shelter- most often this is the underside of a [[Corsair's Balconies|Corsair's Balcony]] and move to it before the torrent of day comes in full.

Another example of extreme adaptation is the '[[Riley's Blunderbuss]]'. The downpour from The Titans is enough that most plants would drown during the day, but during the night may freeze if they hold on to too much water. So some plants simply develop roots above the surface similar to Mangrove trees to avoid drowning and to not stockpile so much water they freeze solid during the night and die.

Riley's Blunderbuss, though, prefers a more violent use of its water content. A massive hollow chamber sits below the plant in the ground. This chamber is full of small pockets that shape water into dulled shards. Fibrous membranes serve to quickly separate these shards when needed, and they are then transported up within mere moments to one of several holes peaking from behind the leaves of the shrub. This reaction is caused by receptors that detect damage to the plant's outer leaves; often caused by an herbivore taking a bite. Once a bite is taken out, the shards of ice are brought forwards to these "barrel" like structures and then loosed with extreme force. as inner mechanisms smush the projectile out at great speed, shattering it slightly on the way out.

While its not likely to be lethal to most [[Monsters]] of The Weald, the blood loss and possibly eye damage means that it become far easier for predators to find anything wounded by a Blunderbuss.

Another example of a plant that lures in predators would be the [[Vissouvois]]. The Vissouvois, pronounced 'Vee-oh-vua', uses hairs along its stalks to detect the heartbeat of creatures, with even a moments glance causing the plant to retract these stalks. If contact is sustained, helve-hammer like structures inside the plant will mimic the destress call of [[Creeping Toads|Creeping Toad Tadpoles]]. Bringing either adult toads or even their predators the [[Man-Fishers]] around to investigate- though it's likely that Man-Fishers may be smart enough to know that its a Vissouvois' distress call and not a tadpoles... I'd really like to get a specimen for study I think. But as said again and again Man-Fishers are an enigma.

Most other plants lean towards carnivory to supplement energy. [[Surgin' Surgeons]], [[Rippervines]], [[Splinter Leaves]] and the like. Rippervines are a noteworthy carnivorous plant specimen, though. With their dense, hefty vines the plant dangles down from the boughs of the [[Weeping Titans]]. Being one of very few non-luminescent plants in The Weald it can be easy to miss them even if they're mere inches away from your face. These vines are made in a pentagonal pattern, with five vines descending downwards with a decently sized gap in the middle of them, enough for a [[Minotaurs|Minotaur]] to stand without touching the vines if in the correct position and clenching their gut in.

If contact is repeatedly made with these vines, the whole network will tense up and close inwards. Sharp hooks will dig their way into the flesh of normal fauna, and will even pierce and cling onto softer-metaled armor such as bronze or iron if the adventurer is unlucky enough. The spikes will release a powerful paralytic and begin rapidly twisting and contorting to completely snare the prey item. These movements will further maim the creature inside, causing immense amounts of blood to be released. Most of this will be absorbed by the plant, but a great quantity will also escape the vines and fall down below as the creature is dragged upwards.

The source of these vines is a large mound with a central orifice. But we can discuss more of that in the [[Rippervines|Rippervine's]] documentations.

And that's it- for a general overview at least. The Sorrowed Weald was the first Region I visited during my first expedition into [[The West]]. I must say it remains haunting, but beautiful in my memories of it as I write this.

The Creatures that can be found inside of The Weald are Extreme Specialists, though the further you get from the beating heart, so to speak, at the forest's center the less they need to conform.

- [[Man-Fishers]]: Native. Ever stalking parties and picking off stragglers, Man-Fishers have long learned that [[adventurers]] are easy prey. No one has ever seen so much as a hair from one of these horrors.
- [[Creeping Toads]]: Native. They sing their hammer-like song at dawn and dusk to find a mate, and use their lacerated tongues to hook onto prey and adventurers alike to drag them into their waiting gullet.
- [[Riphawgs]]: Native. The premiere tracking predators of The Weald, capable of tracking other fauna through the dense rains of day and earning enough meat to sleep at night.
- [[Perfections]]: Spawn. Ever shifting eldritch forms of hatred that mutter about beauty and charity. If one approaches look directly at it, no matter how much it hurts. They're offended if you look away.
- [[Errikxgizes]]: Native. Chittering horrors of insectoids that make calls so loud it rumbles the bones. Elven for "No Harmony" essentially, though it has been misspelled by humans, and that is the current spelling.
- [[Warhelms]]: Native. Known for charging head first at threats and mashing them to paste. Bring some jam along so you might distract one.
- [[The Crows In Black]]: Automata. Malfunctioning mechanized men made for as of yet unknown purposes. They patrol or rest forever inside of the near-sunken ruins within The Weald.
- [[Ouberinth]]: Native. Pale white scavengers with red eyes. While tame enough they should not be approached regardless. Elven for "Of Ivory."
- [[Grolles]]: Mutant Spawn. Honestly, looking at the desired environment of a Grolle it's quite possible that The Weald may in fact be their native region. It's impossible to tell though, so for now it can only be speculated.
- [[Walking Bastions]]: Native. The largest devoted Herbivores in [[The West]], renowned for their densely armored shells and organic flechette cannons that strip away the flora in their path. To be caught in such an attack is to be blasted apart into tiny bits.
- [[Noctollithi]]: Roamer. Aeronautical Osteichthyes resembling large squids, related closely to [[Carrion Thieves]]: Passive creatures that would sooner bolt away from an adventurer than take a bite from one.
- [[Ossuary Titans]]: Roamer. Come frequently to The Weald to cool over a longer period than simply during the night. The heads of the most ancient Titans can peak above the leaves to help them leave when they see fit.
- [[Ghouls]]: Mutant, Roamer. Victims of [[Blightwater]] often seek out The Weald innately, with many succumbing to [[Rippervines]] as they journey towards the uncharted center of the forest. The Native Fauna of The Weald do not predate on Mutant adventurers.
- [[Death Knights]]: Mutant, Roamer. Can be found idle on the surface letting rain wash over them, likely offering some sort of comfort to their affliction. They are often made victim of by [[Man-Fishers]]- found impaled and immolated by the monster's magics.
- [[Immolators]]: Spawn, Invasive. Try as they might, the creatures of The Weald do not take kindly to their presence and many Immolator husks have been found already killed. This place is horrifyingly protective of itself.
- [[Monoliths]]: Spawn, Roamer. Cloaking masses of shadow that knows enough to keep its distance from the iron-taloned predators of The Weald. Will happily make a possession out of a 'vacancy' though if the opportunity presents itself.
- [[Interrogators]]: Mutant, Spawn, Native. To my knowledge the only region-locked eldritch spawn within [[The West]]. Interrogators pin down adventurers for minutes and pry into their brains, leaving with information that is forgotten by the adventurer themself. Don't let them get their hands on you and you'll be fine.
- [[Castellans]]: Native. Massive, four legged arthropods that luckily for us only consume fruit. They often loom near adventurers and snatch up rations when the opportunity arises. Adventurers name them thus because of their tendency to ward off predators in the region with their ethereal song.
- [[Swamp Hulks]]: Native. The bigger cousins of [[Mire Hulks]]. Swamp Hulks are shockingly intelligent, showing signs of complex speech between specimens, though it's impossible to know for sure.
- [[Gyrn's Dooms]]: Native. Horrific mutant centipedes that spray acid from their wounds and have incisors large enough to snap a man in twain. Earned their namesake for the same reasons as the infamous [[Chrozhran Destroyers]], only a Centaur names Gyrn was clearly the victim in this case.
- [[Arcanosaurs]]: Roamer, lightly invasive. Only patrolling the outer edges of The Weald, Arcanosaurs opportunistically consume lone [[Ouberinth]] or other prey items.
- [[Cerulean Whimseys|Cerulean]], [[Teal Whimseys|Teal]], [[Umbral Whimseys|Umbral]], [[Crimson Whimseys|Crimson]] and [[Verdant Whimseys]]: Roamers and Native. Harmless fae that couldn't smite even a fly if they wanted, but may still cause mild or extreme confusion if contact is made for brief moments.
- [[Ents]]: Elementals, appear where [[Dendromancy]] is at its strongest. Hulking masses of roots that strike down those who abuse the forest. Scared off by open flame.
- [[Adamant Reapers]]: Native. The largest of the Reaper family, Their poison is strong enough to drop a [[True Wyverns|True Wyvern]] in merely an hour. Their shells require the force of a [[Dawnhammers|Dawnhammer's]] cannon to pierce.
- [[Bloat Kings]]: Native-ish. Arrives as an adolescent to feast on larger quarry and leaves upon reaching a desired weight.
- [[Vren]]: Native. The token pollinators of The Weald, the life here would not bee, heh, here without them. See what I did there?
- [[Vell Beetles]]: Native. Onery, defensive little bugs that zap anything that touches them. Favored as companions by Vren.
- [[Skaktah]]: Native. Spider-equivalent creatures that feed on the smaller lifeforms within The Weald.
- [[Abominations]]: Mutant, Spawn, Invasive. Even the mighty Abomination, infamous for their durability is unmatched to the devastating power of a [[Man-Fishers|Man-Fisher's]] harpoon. If encountered, it may be best to purposefully scream in a veil of agony to attract a Man-Fisher that it will smite away the fiend in your stead.
- [[Fog Men]]: Mildly Invasive. Creeps along the outskirts of The Weald for the same reasons as an Arcanosaur would, though Fog Men are far more suited to delving deep into the bramble as it were.
- [[Maws]]: Invasive. Galivants about gobbling up anything they can. Every maw's head has a bounty on it and you would be wise to cash it in.
- [[Shamblers]]: Can be found idle deep inside The Weald, suggesting they may have some origins near the heart of the forest. Many defective Shamblers are picked off by the fauna of The Weald as they move towards [[The City of Wyrms]].
- [[Angels]]: Fae. Whimsical, singing beings whose ethereal forms cannot be touched yet offer a sense of warmth and comforts when a hand waves through them. They only appear close to the center of the forest.
- [[Killfreaks]]: Mutant. The precursor stage of an [[Abominations|Abomination]], they seldom survive long enough to become thus in this place.
- [[Moundweirds]]: Roamer. Hulking masses of dead plant matter concealing a central 'skeleton'. Harvests dead plant matter and creatures before returning to [[The Crimson Expanse]].
- [[Weeping Sprites]]: Elemental, Artificial. Harmless blobs of water that go about dissolving rotting plant matter to clean The Weald. Their numbers are finite and dwindling with the arrival of both [[Maws]] and [[Immolators]].
- [[Rejects]]: Spawn, Invasive. The tyrannical legion belonging to [[The Intra-Boreal Exclusion Zone]], or simply the 'Ibeks' as [[adventurers]] call it. Essentially acting as refactored Shamblers, Rejects are smarter, independently acting soldiers of [[The Four]] that cannot be reasoned with. Kill on sight.
